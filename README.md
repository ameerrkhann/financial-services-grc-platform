## What This Project Is

A Python GRC toolchain simulating a real cybersecurity risk programme for a fictional Canadian federally regulated financial institution. It exists to demonstrate practical GRC engineering — not framework recall, but working tools that produce the artefacts a risk function actually ships: a quantified risk register, a board-ready dashboard, an executive briefing, and an auditable vendor assessment trail.

Every number in this README comes from running the code. Reproduce all of it with one command: `python3 src/database/seed_demo_data.py`.

## Module 1 — NIST CSF 2.0 Assessment Engine

Scores the bank across all six CSF 2.0 functions against a target maturity of 4, records the gaps, and ranks them into a remediation roadmap.

### Current maturity — overall 2.2 / 5.0

| Function | Current | Target | Gap | Status |
|----------|---------|--------|-----|--------|
| Govern | 2/5 | 4/5 | 2 | ⚠️ Gap |
| Identify | 2/5 | 4/5 | 2 | ⚠️ Gap |
| Protect | 3/5 | 4/5 | 1 | ✅ At defined |
| Detect | 2/5 | 4/5 | 2 | ⚠️ Gap |
| Respond | 1/5 | 4/5 | 3 | ⚠️ Gap |
| Recover | 3/5 | 4/5 | 1 | ✅ At defined |

A score below 3 is a gap: 3 means documented and consistently followed, 4 means measured and tracked with metrics.

### Remediation roadmap

Ranked by `(3 − score) × (7 − function priority)`, so a deeper gap in a more foundational function is fixed first.

```
╭─────┬────────────┬─────────┬────────────┬──────────┬─────────────────┬──────────────╮
│   # │ Function   │ Score   │ Priority   │ Effort   │ Est. Fix Time   │   Risk Score │
├─────┼────────────┼─────────┼────────────┼──────────┼─────────────────┼──────────────┤
│   1 │ Govern     │ 2/5     │ Critical   │ Low      │ 4 weeks         │            6 │
│   2 │ Identify   │ 2/5     │ Critical   │ Medium   │ 8 weeks         │            5 │
│   3 │ Respond    │ 1/5     │ High       │ Low      │ 6 weeks         │            4 │
│   4 │ Detect     │ 2/5     │ High       │ Medium   │ 10 weeks        │            3 │
╰─────┴────────────┴─────────┴────────────┴──────────┴─────────────────┴──────────────╯
```

## Module 2 — Quantitative Risk Engine (FAIR)

### Why FAIR over High / Medium / Low

A board cannot act on "ransomware risk is HIGH." It can act on "ransomware carries an expected annual loss of $5.42M, with a 1-in-10 chance of exceeding $13.99M — against $500K a year to control it, a 713% return."

### The five scenarios

100,000 simulated years each, fixed seed per scenario so every artefact in this repo quotes the same number.

| Scenario | Loss range | Events/yr | ALE | 90th %ile | Control cost | Ctrl eff. | Residual ALE | ROSI |
|----------|-----------|-----------|-----|-----------|--------------|-----------|--------------|------|
| Ransomware Attack | $800K – $12M | 0.5–2.0 | **$5.42M** | $13.99M | $500K | 75% | $1.35M | 713% |
| Data Breach — Customer PII | $500K – $8M | 0.5–3.0 | **$4.97M** | $11.74M | $350K | 70% | $1.49M | 894% |
| Cloud Misconfiguration | $150K – $6M | 1.0–4.0 | **$4.46M** | $10.24M | $180K | 80% | $892.8K | 1884% |
| Third-Party Vendor Failure | $400K – $9M | 0.3–1.5 | **$2.70M** | $7.63M | $200K | 60% | $1.08M | 711% |
| Insider Threat | $200K – $5M | 0.5–2.0 | **$2.02M** | $5.27M | $280K | 65% | $706.0K | 368% |
| **Portfolio** | | | **$19.58M** | | **$1.51M** | | **$5.53M** | **830%** |

The full control programme costs $1.51M a year and removes $14.05M of expected annual loss — 72% of the portfolio.

**Control effectiveness is an assumption, not a measurement.** Each scenario documents a 60–80% figure in [`scenarios.py`](src/risk_quantification/scenarios.py) reflecting a mature-but-imperfect control stack. It is the number a sceptical audit committee should challenge first, and lowering it reduces ROSI proportionately.

### Tail risk

| Scenario | Median | 95th %ile | P(> $1M) | P(> $5M) |
|----------|--------|-----------|----------|----------|
| Ransomware Attack | $3.27M | $18.70M | 68% | 39% |
| Data Breach — Customer PII | $3.47M | $15.26M | 76% | 38% |
| Cloud Misconfiguration | $2.96M | $13.91M | 77% | 31% |
| Third-Party Vendor Failure | $1.01M | $11.02M | 50% | 18% |
| Insider Threat | $1.04M | $7.41M | 51% | 11% |

![Loss Exceedance Curves — All Scenarios](dashboards/lec_all_scenarios.png)

### How the model works

Each simulated year draws an event count `N ~ Poisson(λ)`, then draws **N independent** log-normal loss magnitudes and sums them. Loss low and high are treated as the 5th and 95th percentiles of the magnitude distribution; λ is the midpoint of the frequency range. Vectorised with `np.repeat` and `np.bincount` — no Python loop over 100,000 years.

> **This was wrong for several weeks.** The first version multiplied a single loss draw by the event count, forcing every event in a year to cost the same and inflating the 99th percentile by 22–36%. The mean was unaffected, which is why it survived review. [`notebooks/README.md`](notebooks/README.md) documents the bug, the fix, and the before/after numbers; [`notebooks/01_fair_first_attempt.py`](notebooks/01_fair_first_attempt.py) preserves the original code.

`riskquant` is **not** a dependency — it does not support Python 3.13, so the FAIR maths is implemented directly with numpy in [`fair_engine.py`](src/risk_quantification/fair_engine.py).

## Module 3 — Vendor Risk Assessor (OSFI B-10)

A Streamlit app that generates a questionnaire tailored to the vendor's service type, scores the responses with a weighted engine, assigns a risk tier, and writes a full audit trail to SQLite.

### Questionnaire coverage

14 universal questions plus 4 service-specific ones per vendor type — 18 questions across Cloud Provider, Payment Processor, Software / SaaS, Data Analytics / AI, and IT Infrastructure. Weight-3 questions (policy, certification, breach notification, MFA, encryption, BCP) move the score more than weight-2 ones. `N/A` is excluded from the denominator rather than scored as zero.

### Risk tiers and reassessment cycles

| Score | Tier | Reassessment | Action required |
|-------|------|--------------|-----------------|
| 0–40 | 🔴 Critical | 90 days | Do not onboard without a remediation plan. Executive approval required. |
| 41–60 | 🟠 High | 90 days | Onboard with conditions. Quarterly reassessment. |
| 61–80 | 🟡 Medium | 365 days | Standard onboarding. Annual reassessment. |
| 81–100 | 🟢 Low | 730 days | Approve for onboarding. Biennial reassessment. |

The cycle is defined once, in `RISK_TIERS[...]["reassessment_days"]`, and both `get_overdue_vendors()` and the Power BI export read it — so the cycle shown to an assessor is the cycle actually enforced.

## Methodology and Limitations

**FAIR.** Monte Carlo with log-normal loss magnitude and Poisson event frequency. Loss low/high are the 5th and 95th percentiles of a 90% confidence interval; λ is the midpoint of the frequency range. Each simulated year sums N independent magnitude draws. Full detail in [`docs/risk_assessment_methodology.md`](docs/risk_assessment_methodology.md).

**Known limitations, stated plainly:**

1. **Fictional organisation.** All parameters are illustrative. A real assessment needs organisation-specific threat intelligence and historical loss data.
2. **Scenarios are modelled independently.** In practice one attack triggers several at once — ransomware causing both operational disruption and a data breach.
3. **Control effectiveness is an assumption.** The 60–80% values driving residual ALE and ROSI are judgements, not measurements from control testing.
4. **Static parameters.** The threat landscape moves; these should be reviewed annually or after any significant incident.
5. **λ is a midpoint.** The frequency range is treated as a plausible band for the annual event rate, not as distribution percentiles.

## Regulatory Framework

Built for the Canadian federally regulated financial institution (FRFI) environment.

| Guideline / advisory | Governs | Key dates | Addressed by |
|----------------------|---------|-----------|--------------|
| **OSFI B-13** — Technology and Cyber Risk Management | Technology and cyber risk across three domains: Governance and Risk Management; Technology Operations and Resilience; Cyber Security | Effective **1 January 2024** | CSF maturity scoring, FAIR engine, loss exceedance curves |
| **OSFI B-10** — Third-Party Risk Management | Third-party risk, proportionate to criticality | Revised 2023, effective **1 May 2024** | Module 3 vendor risk assessor |
| **OSFI E-21** — Operational Risk and Resilience Management | Operational resilience, critical operations, scenario testing | Full adherence expected by **1 September 2026**; scenario testing of all critical operations expected by **1 September 2027** | Recover function scoring, BCP gap analysis, severe scenario modelling |
| **Technology and Cyber Security Incident Reporting** advisory | Reporting a reportable incident to OSFI **within 24 hours** | In effect | Respond function remediation, control RS.CO-02 |
| **ISO/IEC 27001:2022** | International information security standard, 93 Annex A controls in 4 themes | — | Cross-framework control mapping |
| **SOC 2** | Trust Services Criteria | — | Cross-framework control mapping |

> The 24-hour reporting clock comes from OSFI's separate *Technology and Cyber Security Incident Reporting* advisory, **not** from B-13 — a distinction this project originally got wrong. B-13 also does not impose a Canadian data-residency rule; B-10 sets the expectation that a FRFI knows and controls where its data is stored and accessed.

## Why Financial Services

I work as a Data Analyst at Manulife and part-time at Scotiabank. Both are OSFI-regulated. This project demonstrates GRC skills in the exact regulatory environment Canadian financial institutions operate in.

OSFI E-21 full adherence was due 1 September 2026, and scenario testing across all critical operations is expected by 1 September 2027. Canadian FRFIs are actively building out that testing capability now, which is driving demand for exactly the kind of quantified, evidence-producing risk tooling this project implements.
